## 2. Decisions already taken (ask the lead only if one cannot work; say why)

1. **A bridge is a street.** Each bridge adds one `Street(kind="bridge")` to `plan.streets`, appended right after `lanes` and after `link_banks`, so every earlier street keeps its index; the record goes to `CityPlan.bridges` with its street's index. Its line runs from the center line of one bank's street to the other's, so `export/streets.center_lines` makes the junction on the quay by itself. Add `"bridge"` to `STREET_LIFT`, a little under the quay's lift so the end of its ribbon stays under the quay's asphalt and paint. Grep every use of `st.kind`, `plan.streets`, `Dresser.surfaces`, `walk_widths`: nothing may park, plant or light a bridge by accident (`parking` line 510 reads `empty.get(kind, 0.0)`, so an unknown kind is never left empty: check it).
2. **Axis.** Keep a crossing's own chord when it is within `[bridges] skew` degrees (about 30) of the river's normal at the point it crosses the river (it continues the border's arterial); `connector`s always keep theirs. Otherwise the axis is the river's normal through that point, from the first street on one bank to the first on the other. A bridge never crosses a block, a building or another street. If two crossings come within `[bridges] gap` m along the river, keep the higher one (connector, arterial, street), and report how many.
3. **Enough bridges.** Prompt 61 left a crossing only where something was cut: the capital has one for 1.7 km of river. Add bridges so that no stretch of river inside a riverside town or city with streets on both banks runs more than `[bridges] every` m (about 500) between bridges or from one to the end of those streets. A new bridge goes where a street meets a quay, best on both banks; it may end in mid-block on one quay. They are `Crossing(line, "street", width)` appended to `plan.crossings`, so every crossing in the list has a bridge.
4. **Order in `plan_city`.** Riverside walks move after the bridges: `river.riverside_walks` already cuts its band with `streets`, so a walk and its tree row stop at the bridge without a new rule. Nothing between uses `walks`. Check that `plan/*/buildings`, `households`, `souks`, `landmarks`, `districts`, `port` and `props` stay byte-identical.
5. **Heights.** The carve does not change and neither does `Ground.height`. Along the axis (s in meters):
   - `ends`: where the ribbon leaves its street (the edge of a quay's surface) or, on a country road, the ground. Beyond them the ribbon follows `Ground.height` as today.
   - Required height at each s: the ground; over the water `River.surface + clearance + deck depth`. The profile is the **least concave majorant** of those points: a straight line where the banks are high enough (the prompt's "straight line between the abutments"), a straight ramp up from each end and a level top where they are not. A city bridge's ends are fixed; a country bridge's ends move outward until its ramps meet `[bridges] grade`.
   - `clearance` is `[min, per meter of water width, max]` (a stream 3 m wide does not need what a river 30 m wide does). `grade` the steepest ramp. With quays this low a small city bridge cannot have both, so grade wins, down to `clearance_min` (about 0.9 m of air); if even that fails, say how many and how steep. Round the crown a little over a few meters without going under the required height. Bars on quick and 64 km: air over water at least `clearance_min` at every wet point of every bridge; grade under 0.10 on all but the listed exceptions.
   - The deck is level across. The ribbon meets the quay or road with no step over 3 cm.
   - `Ground.surface` is "the top of what is drawn": on a city deck it returns the deck, so anything standing there is right; `Ground.height` stays the ground, `Ground.lift` is not to be read on a deck. Keep the extra cost out of the common path (no deck near: no cost).
6. **The record.** `Bridge` in `city/model.py` holds all a mesher needs and nothing else: kind, axis, width, street index, `ends`, `span` (the open part, abutment to abutment: outside it the ramp is solid fill), the profile as arrays, the water level and the channel's s range, the pier or arch stations, the river id, whether it is dry. Round 2 draws the structure (arches, beams, piers, parapets, ramps, lod1) from this record alone, with no other change: design for that.
7. **Kinds.** `arch` (stone) for local and regional country roads, for `street` crossings and for any bridge with an end by the old town (`[bridges] old_reach`); `beam` (concrete) for national roads, arterials and connectors. An arch only up to `[bridges] arch_max` m of span. Number of arches or spans from the open length (`arch_span`, `beam_span`), odd for arches, a pier never in the water of a stream narrower than `pier_water`.
8. **A dry oued.** On a country road: a paved ford, `kind="ford"` (the Maghreb's radier: a concrete slab across the gravel, marker posts in round 2). The ribbon there is concrete (`quay` or `concrete`, an existing material), with ramps down the low banks at `grade`: the ground dilated upward at that slope, never dug. In a town or city: a low `beam` bridge, `clearance_min` over the gravel.
9. **Country bridges.** `world` and `world.json` do not change. Computed from `world.roads` and the rivers when a tile asks, cached per road on `TileContext` (a box build must not pay for the whole country), a pure function of the road, the rivers and `Ground`, so two tiles agree on a shared edge. A crossing inside `tc.city_area` is the city's. `road_meshes` and lod1's `road_ribbons` take the deck's height there; vertices at `ends`, `span` and each kink, or the ribbon cuts the corners.
10. **Materials.** Reuse the palette (`quay_wall`, `plinth_stone`, `concrete`, `railing_white`, `iron`, `curb`, `sidewalk`). `kit/*`, `world` and `gazetteer` must not change.
11. **Maps.** `export/maps.py`: a city map marks each bridge (the deck drawn over the river with two dark edges), a country map each bridge and ford by kind, at sizes that read at 1800 px. `plan.json` gains `bridges`; `streets.json` shows them as edges of kind `bridge` with their real z at the nodes.
